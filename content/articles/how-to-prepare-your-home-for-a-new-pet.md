To prepare your home for a new pet, **start about a week early, get down to pet level to look for dangers, put away cords, cleaners, medicines, and toxic plants, and set up a safe space with a bed or crate, food and water bowls, and toys.** A few hours of prep makes the first days calmer and much safer for your new dog or cat.

Pets explore with their mouths and paws. Anything they can reach, they may chew, swallow, or knock over.

## Step 1: Do a "pet-eye" walk-through

The AAHA suggests doing a safety check at your pet's level. Get down on your hands and knees and look around each room. You will spot things you never notice standing up, like loose cords, small toys, coins, and gaps behind furniture.

Do it once when you start and again after you set everything up.

## Step 2: Remove common dangers

| Danger | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical cords | Chewing can cause burns or shocks | Hide cords or use cord covers |
| Cleaning products | Can poison or burn | Store high or behind latched doors |
| Human medicines | Even one pill can be dangerous | Keep in closed cabinets, never on counters |
| Toxic foods | Chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, xylitol | Keep off counters and tables |
| Small objects | Choking and blockage risk | Pick up hair ties, coins, socks, toys |
| Trash cans | Food scraps, bones, wrappers | Use cans with locking lids |
| Toxic plants | Can cause vomiting or worse | Remove or move out of reach |
| Strings and yarn | Dangerous if swallowed, especially for cats | Put away sewing and craft items |

## Step 3: Pet-proof room by room

### Kitchen

- Use childproof latches on lower cabinets.
- Keep food, knives, and sponges off the edges of counters.
- Store trash under the sink or use a locking can.

### Living room

- Hide or cover cords behind furniture.
- Move breakable items and candles up high.
- Check under couches for small things pets could swallow.
- Tie up window blind cords.

### Bathroom

- Keep the toilet lid down. Pets may drink from it, and cleaning tablets can be toxic.
- Store medicines, razors, and cotton swabs in closed cabinets.

### Bedrooms

- Keep shoes, socks, and hair ties off the floor.
- Close closet doors.
- Put laundry in a basket with a lid.

### Garage and laundry room

- Store antifreeze, pesticides, paint, and rodent poison up high. Antifreeze tastes sweet to pets and is very dangerous.
- Keep the dryer and washer doors closed, and always check inside before starting a load.

### Yard

- Check fences for gaps and loose boards.
- Remove toxic plants and mushrooms.
- Keep pools covered or fenced.
- Store lawn and garden chemicals safely.

## Step 4: Check your plants

Many common houseplants can make pets sick. VCA and the ASPCA list plants like **pothos, aloe vera, sago palm, and lilies** as dangerous. **Lilies are especially dangerous for cats**, since even pollen or vase water can cause kidney failure.

Before your pet arrives, check each plant on the ASPCA's list of toxic and non-toxic plants, and move or remove anything risky.

## Step 5: Set up a safe space

Your pet needs a quiet place to rest and feel safe, especially in the first few days.

- **Dogs:** a crate or small gated area with a bed, water, and a few chew toys
- **Cats:** a quiet room with a litter box, bed, food, water, a scratching post, and hiding spots

Keep the litter box away from food and water bowls.

## New pet supply checklist

**For dogs:**

- Food and water bowls
- Food recommended by the shelter, breeder, or vet
- Collar, ID tag, and leash
- Crate and bed
- Chew toys
- Poop bags
- Brush and nail clippers
- Baby gates

**For cats:**

- Food and water bowls
- Cat food
- Litter box, litter, and scoop
- Scratching post
- Carrier
- Bed and toys
- Collar with ID tag

## Step 6: Plan the first vet visit

- **Book a vet visit** in the first week.
- **Ask about vaccines,** flea and tick prevention, and spay or neuter.
- **Get a microchip** if your pet does not have one, and register it with your contact details.
- **Save emergency numbers,** including your vet, the nearest 24-hour animal hospital, and the **ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435**.

## Step 7: Get your home ready for pet hair and messes

- **Pick easy-clean fabrics** or use washable covers on furniture.
- **Keep enzyme cleaner handy** for accidents, since it removes odors so pets do not return to the same spot.
- **Vacuum often.** Pet hair and dander add to household dust. Our guide on [why a house gets so dusty](/why-is-my-house-so-dusty) has easy tips.
- **Keep food bowls clean** so fruit flies and gnats do not show up. See our guide on [getting rid of gnats](/how-to-get-rid-of-gnats).

## The bottom line

Prepare your home for a new pet about a week ahead. Walk through at pet level, put away cords, cleaners, medicines, small objects, and toxic foods and plants, and use latches and gates where needed. Set up a quiet safe space, buy the basics, and book a vet visit in the first week. Keep the ASPCA Poison Control number, (888) 426-4435, and your vet's number where you can find them fast.
