Percentage change tells you how much something has gone up or down compared with where it started. It’s the number you need to understand a price rise, compare discounts, track savings or check exam scores.

The calculation takes three steps. If you just want the answer, our free [percentage calculator](/tools/percentage-calculator) does it for you and shows the working.

## The percentage change formula

**Percentage change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100**

1. **Subtract** the old value from the new value.
2. **Divide** the result by the old value.
3. **Multiply** by 100 to turn it into a percentage.

If the answer is positive, it’s a percentage **increase**. If it’s negative, it’s a percentage **decrease**.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: A price increase

Your monthly phone bill goes from 40 to 46.

- 46 − 40 = 6
- 6 ÷ 40 = 0.15
- 0.15 × 100 = **15% increase**

### Example 2: A price decrease

A jacket drops from 80 to 60 in a sale.

- 60 − 80 = −20
- −20 ÷ 80 = −0.25
- −0.25 × 100 = **−25%, a 25% decrease**

### Example 3: Test scores

A score rises from 64 to 72.

- 72 − 64 = 8
- 8 ÷ 64 = 0.125
- 0.125 × 100 = **12.5% increase**

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Dividing by the wrong number

Always divide by the **original** (old) value, not the new one. From 80 to 60 is a 25% decrease, but from 60 to 80 is a 33.3% increase — the same difference of 20 is a bigger share of 60 than of 80.

### Expecting percentages to cancel out

A 50% increase followed by a 50% decrease does **not** take you back to where you started. 100 + 50% = 150, then 150 − 50% = 75. Each percentage applies to a different starting value.

### Mixing up percent and percentage points

If an interest rate goes from 4% to 5%, it has risen by **1 percentage point** — but by **25%** in relative terms (1 ÷ 4 × 100). News reports often blur the two, so check which one is meant.

### Starting from zero

Percentage change from zero is undefined, because you can’t divide by zero. If a value goes from 0 to 10, describe the actual change instead.

## Related calculations

### Finding a percentage of a number

To find 15% of 240: 15 ÷ 100 × 240 = **36**.

### What percent one number is of another

To find what percent 18 is of 60: 18 ÷ 60 × 100 = **30%**.

### Working backwards to the original price

If something costs 68 after a 15% discount, the original price is 68 ÷ (1 − 0.15) = 68 ÷ 0.85 = **80**. (Taking 15% off 68 and adding it back gives the wrong answer.)

## Quick reference

| --- | --- |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| X% of Y | X ÷ 100 × Y |
| X is what % of Y | X ÷ Y × 100 |
| Original before a discount of D% | price ÷ (1 − D ÷ 100) |
| Original before an increase of I% | price ÷ (1 + I ÷ 100) |
