A carved pumpkin usually rots in **3 to 7 days**, because cutting it open lets in air, mold, and bacteria. To make it last longer, **carve it just a few days before Halloween, scrape the inside clean, spray it with a weak bleach solution, seal the cut edges, and keep it in a cool, shady spot.** With good care, a jack-o'-lantern can last 2 weeks or more.

Below are 9 tips that really help, plus the myths that do not.

## Why carved pumpkins rot so fast

A whole pumpkin can last for months. Its thick skin keeps germs out. Once you cut it open:

- **Mold and bacteria** get into the soft flesh.
- **The cut edges dry out**, so the pumpkin shrinks and wrinkles.
- **Warm weather** helps mold grow faster.
- **Bugs**, like fruit flies and gnats, are drawn to the sugary flesh.
- **Squirrels and other animals** like to nibble on it.

Most tips work by slowing one of these problems down.

## How long carved pumpkins last

| Conditions | How long it may last |
|---|---|
| Hot, sunny, or rainy weather | 2 to 4 days |
| Typical fall weather, no special care | 3 to 7 days |
| Cool weather, cleaned and sealed | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Cool, dry, kept in shade and treated often | Up to 3 weeks or more |

Where you live matters a lot. A pumpkin on a Florida porch will not last as long as one in Minnesota.

## 1. Pick a fresh, healthy pumpkin

The longer your pumpkin was healthy before carving, the longer it will last after.

- **Choose one with a green, firm stem.** A dry or missing stem can mean it was picked long ago.
- **Press the skin.** It should be hard everywhere, with no soft spots.
- **Check the bottom.** Soft or dark spots mean rot has already started.
- **Avoid cuts and bruises** on the skin.
- **Carry it from the bottom,** not by the stem. A broken stem lets rot in.

## 2. Carve it at the right time

Carve your pumpkin about **3 to 5 days before Halloween**. You can buy it earlier and leave it whole on the porch. Just keep it out of the hot sun and off wet ground.

Want to plan it out? Our [Halloween countdown](/tools/days-until-halloween) shows how many days you have left.

## 3. Scrape the inside really clean

Seeds and stringy pulp rot first. Use a large metal spoon or an ice cream scoop to scrape the inside walls until they are smooth. Try to leave the walls about **1 inch thick**. Thin walls dry out and cave in faster.

**Tip:** Cut the opening in the **bottom or back** instead of the top. A lid cut from the top lets the stem area rot faster, and a bottom opening lets you set the pumpkin over a candle or light.

## 4. Use a weak bleach solution

Bleach kills the mold and bacteria that cause rot. This is one of the most helpful steps.

1. Mix **1 tablespoon of bleach** in **1 gallon of water**.
2. Spray the inside and all the cut surfaces, or soak the whole pumpkin for a few minutes.
3. Let it **air dry** completely, cut side down, on a towel.
4. Spray again every day or two.

Wear gloves, work in a place with fresh air, and keep children and pets away until it dries. Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia, since that makes harmful fumes.

## 5. Seal the cut edges

After the pumpkin is clean and dry, rub a **thin layer of petroleum jelly** on all the cut edges. This keeps them from drying out and shriveling. Some people use vegetable oil instead.

Use only a thin layer. A thick coat can trap moisture, which mold likes. If you see mold, wipe the jelly off, spray with bleach water again, and let it dry.

## 6. Keep it cool, shady, and dry

Heat and sun are the biggest enemies of a carved pumpkin.

- **Place it in the shade**, out of direct afternoon sun.
- **Keep it off wet ground.** Set it on a plate, board, or mat.
- **Cover it or bring it inside** when it rains.
- **Bring it in on very warm days**, then put it back out in the evening.

Do not leave it next to a heater or in a warm, sunny window indoors, either.

## 7. Give it a cold soak or a night in the fridge

If your pumpkin starts to look wrinkled, you can often bring it back.

1. Fill a sink or bucket with **cold water**.
2. Soak the pumpkin face down for **1 to 8 hours**.
3. Take it out, dry it well, and spray it with bleach water again.

You can also put a carved pumpkin in a plastic bag in the **refrigerator overnight**. The cold slows mold and helps it firm up.

## 8. Use battery lights instead of candles

A real candle heats the inside and basically cooks the pumpkin, which makes it soften faster. **Battery tea lights or LED lights** stay cool and are safer, too. Real flames near costumes, leaves, and kids can start a fire.

If you do use a real candle, never leave it burning when no one is around.

## 9. Keep bugs and animals away

- **Fruit flies and gnats** love the sweet flesh. A clean, dry pumpkin attracts fewer bugs. If they show up in your house, see our guides on [trapping fruit flies at home](/how-to-get-rid-of-fruit-flies) and [stopping gnats](/how-to-get-rid-of-gnats).
- **Squirrels** may chew on pumpkins. Some people sprinkle cayenne pepper on the outside, but it washes off in the rain.

## Myths that do not help much

| Myth | The truth |
|---|---|
| "Hairspray seals the pumpkin" | It may add a little shine, but it does not stop mold, and it is flammable near candles. |
| "Lemon juice keeps it fresh" | It works for apples, but it does little against mold on a pumpkin. |
| "Carve early to get the best design" | You can do it early, but it will likely be mushy by Halloween. |
| "Plastic wrap keeps it fresh outside" | Wrapping traps moisture and can speed up mold outdoors. Only use a bag in the fridge. |

## No-carve ideas that last much longer

If you want decorations that last all season, skip the knife. A whole pumpkin can last **2 to 3 months** in cool weather.

- **Paint** faces or patterns with acrylic paint.
- **Use stickers** or push-in decorating kits.
- **Draw** on it with markers.
- **Stack** a few small pumpkins into a pumpkin "snowman."
- **Carve a fake pumpkin** made of foam, which you can keep for years.

## What to do with your pumpkin after Halloween

- **Compost it** if you have a compost pile. Remove candles and any jelly or wax first.
- **Bury it** in the garden, where it will break down and feed the soil.
- **Check for local pumpkin drop-offs.** Some towns collect pumpkins for compost.
- **Do not eat a carved pumpkin** that has been sitting out. Bacteria and mold may have grown inside.

Halloween also comes right before the clocks change. See [when the clocks go back this year](/when-do-clocks-change) so you are not caught off guard.

## The bottom line

A carved pumpkin usually lasts 3 to 7 days. To help it last longer, pick a firm, fresh pumpkin, carve it only 3 to 5 days before Halloween, scrape the inside clean, and spray it with a weak bleach solution. Seal the cut edges with a thin layer of petroleum jelly, keep it in a cool, shady, dry spot, and use battery lights instead of candles. If it starts to shrivel, a cold water soak or a night in the fridge can bring it back.
