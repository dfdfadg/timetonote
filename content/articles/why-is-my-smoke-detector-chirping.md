A smoke detector chirps because **it is trying to tell you something needs attention**. Most of the time, one short chirp every 30 to 60 seconds means the battery is low. If it keeps chirping after you change the battery, the alarm usually needs to be reset, or it is old and needs to be replaced.

The good news is that you can fix most chirping alarms in less than 5 minutes. You just need the right battery, a step stool, and a few simple steps.

This guide explains what each type of chirp means, how to stop it, and when it is time for a new alarm.

## First, is it a chirp or an alarm?

A chirp and a real alarm sound very different. Knowing which one you hear tells you how fast you need to act.

| Sound | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| One short chirp every 30 to 60 seconds | Low battery | Change the battery |
| Chirps that do not stop after a new battery | Error, or the alarm is old | Reset it, then replace it if needed |
| 3 long beeps, a pause, then repeat | **Smoke or fire** | Get everyone out now and call 911 |
| 4 beeps, a pause, then repeat (on a CO alarm) | **Carbon monoxide** | Get outside to fresh air and call 911 |

If you hear a loud pattern of 3 or 4 beeps, do not treat it as a chirp. Leave the house first and figure it out later.

## 8 reasons your smoke detector is chirping

### 1. The battery is low

This is the most common cause by far. The alarm chirps once every 30 to 60 seconds to warn you before the battery dies. The chirp can go on for days, so it is easy to miss at first.

**Fix:** Put in a new battery of the exact type printed on the alarm. Many use a 9-volt battery, and others use AA batteries. Do not use rechargeable batteries unless the alarm says they are okay.

### 2. The battery drawer is not closed

Some alarms have a small battery drawer or door. If it is not fully shut, the battery may not touch the metal contacts, and the alarm keeps chirping.

**Fix:** Open the drawer, check that the battery sits the right way, and press it closed until it clicks.

### 3. The alarm needs to be reset

Many alarms remember a low-battery warning even after you put in a new battery. They keep chirping until the memory clears.

**Fix:** Follow these steps to reset most alarms:

1. Take the alarm off the ceiling mount. Most twist off to the left.
2. If it is hardwired, unplug the power connector on the back.
3. Remove the battery.
4. Press and hold the test button for 15 to 20 seconds. This drains any leftover power.
5. Put the battery back in and reconnect the power.
6. Twist the alarm back onto its mount and press the test button to make sure it works.

### 4. The alarm is at the end of its life

Smoke alarms do not last forever. The sensor inside wears out over time. Many newer alarms have a built-in end-of-life warning that chirps no matter what battery you use.

The NFPA says to **replace smoke alarms every 10 years**. Look for the date printed on the back of the alarm. If it is close to 10 years old, or you cannot find a date at all, replace it.

### 5. It has a sealed 10-year battery

Some alarms have a battery sealed inside that lasts about 10 years. You cannot open these to swap the battery. When the sealed battery gets low, the whole alarm needs to be replaced.

**Fix:** Check the label. If it says "10-year sealed battery" or has no battery door, buy a new alarm.

### 6. The hardwired alarm's backup battery is low

Hardwired alarms connect to your home's power, but most still have a **backup battery** for power outages. When that backup battery gets low, the alarm chirps just like a battery-only alarm.

**Fix:** Turn off the power to the alarms at the breaker, then change the backup battery and reset the alarm as shown above. Turn the power back on when you are done.

### 7. Dust or bugs are inside

Dust, cobwebs, and tiny bugs can get into the sensing chamber. This can cause chirps or even false alarms. If your home tends to get dusty, the alarms collect it too. Our guide on [why homes collect so much dust](/why-is-my-house-so-dusty) can help you cut down on it.

**Fix:** Gently vacuum the outside of the alarm with a soft brush attachment. You can also use a can of compressed air on the vents. Do not open the alarm or spray cleaners into it.

### 8. Temperature or humidity changes

Steam from a shower or cooking can set off alarms or make them chirp. Cold air can also make a weak battery drop in power, which is why chirping often starts at night.

**Fix:** Try to keep smoke alarms away from bathroom doors and at least about 10 feet from the stove. Use the bathroom fan when you shower.

## Why does the chirping start at 2 a.m.?

It feels like every smoke alarm picks the middle of the night to start chirping. There is a simple reason for this.

Most homes are coolest in the early morning hours. A battery that is almost dead makes less power when it is cold. That small drop is enough to trigger the low-battery chirp. When the house warms up in the morning, the chirp may even stop for a while, only to come back the next night.

If an alarm chirps at night and then goes quiet, do not ignore it. Change the battery the next day.

## How to find which alarm is chirping

In a home with many alarms, it can be hard to tell which one is making the noise. The sound bounces off walls, and a chirp is short.

Here are a few tricks:

- **Stand still and wait.** Stand under one alarm for a full minute and listen. Then move to the next one.
- **Look at the lights.** The alarm with the problem often blinks its light in a different way from the others.
- **Check every alarm, including the basement.** It is easy to forget alarms in hallways, garages, and basements.
- **Remember CO alarms.** Carbon monoxide alarms chirp for low batteries and end of life too.

In homes with connected alarms, a chirp usually comes from just one unit, even if all of them are linked. Fix that one first.

## Should you replace all your smoke alarms at once?

If one alarm is at the end of its life, the others were probably put in at the same time. It is often smart to replace them all together. This saves you from more 2 a.m. chirps later.

When you shop, you can choose:

- **Alarms with a 10-year sealed battery.** No battery changes for 10 years.
- **Combination smoke and CO alarms.** One device covers both dangers.
- **Hardwired alarms** if your home already has the wiring for them.
- **Connected alarms** that all sound when one detects smoke.

Make sure the box says the alarm is listed by a testing lab, such as UL.

## How to keep your smoke alarms working

A few quick habits keep your alarms ready and quiet:

- **Test each alarm once a month.** Press the test button until it sounds.
- **Change batteries at least once a year** in alarms with replaceable batteries. Many people do it when the clocks change.
- **Clean them every few months** with a vacuum brush.
- **Write the install date** on each new alarm with a marker.
- **Replace every alarm at 10 years.**

Fall is a great time to do all of this, right before you turn on the heat for the winter. It is also a good time to check your heating system. If your heater is acting up, see [what to do when the furnace blows cold air](/why-is-my-furnace-blowing-cold-air).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Pulling the battery and forgetting it.** An alarm with no battery cannot save your life.
- **Using the wrong battery.** Always match the type printed on the alarm.
- **Skipping the reset.** A new battery alone does not always stop the chirp.
- **Painting over alarms.** Paint can block the sensor.
- **Ignoring CO alarms.** They chirp too, and they matter just as much.

## When to call a pro

Most chirping problems are easy DIY fixes. Call an electrician if:

- Your hardwired alarms keep chirping after new batteries and a reset
- The alarms go off with no smoke and you cannot find a reason
- You want to add new hardwired or connected alarms

## The bottom line

A chirping smoke detector usually means a low battery, a needed reset, or an old alarm. Change the battery, hold the test button for 15 to 20 seconds, and close the battery door firmly. If it still chirps, check the date on the back. Any alarm that is 10 years old or has a sealed battery that is running low should be replaced right away. A quiet, working alarm is one of the easiest ways to keep your family safe.
