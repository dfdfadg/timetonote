When an iPhone won’t charge, the cause is usually something simple: a damaged cable, lint packed into the charging port, or a setting that pauses charging on purpose. Work through the steps below in order — they start with the quickest checks and move towards the ones that need more time.

> [!WARNING]
> If your iPhone, cable or charger is very hot, swollen, damaged, or smells of burning, unplug it immediately and stop using it. Contact Apple Support or an Apple Authorised Service Provider.

## Step 1: Check what’s actually happening

First, work out which problem you have, because the fixes differ:

- **No charging at all** — no charging icon, and the percentage doesn’t rise.
- **Charging very slowly** — the icon appears, but the percentage barely moves.
- **Charging stops at 80%** — often a feature, not a fault (see Step 6).
- **“Accessory may not be supported”** alert — usually the cable, adapter or port.
- **Liquid detected alert** — see Step 4 before doing anything else.

## Step 2: Try a different cable, adapter and power source

Cables fail far more often than iPhones. Bends near the connector, frayed insulation and bent contacts are common.

1. Plug your charger into a different wall socket.
2. Try another cable — ideally Apple’s own, or one that is certified (MFi-certified for Lightning; a reputable USB-C cable for iPhone 15 and later).
3. Try another adapter. Charging from a computer is slower and can stop when the computer goes to sleep.

If the iPhone charges with a different cable or adapter, you’ve found the problem.

## Step 3: Clean the charging port carefully

Pocket lint gets pressed into the port every time you plug in, and eventually the connector can’t seat fully. The tell-tale sign: the cable feels loose, or charging only works when you hold it at an angle.

1. Turn off the iPhone.
2. Shine a torch into the port and look for packed lint.
3. Gently loosen and lift it out with a wooden or plastic toothpick. Don’t use anything metal, and don’t scrape the contacts.
4. Avoid blowing into the port or using liquid cleaners.

Reconnect the cable. It should click in firmly.

## Step 4: If you see a liquid detection alert

Newer iPhones can detect liquid in the port and will stop charging through the cable to protect it. Apple recommends:

1. Unplug the cable from the iPhone and the adapter.
2. Tap the iPhone gently against your hand with the port facing down.
3. Leave it in a dry area with some airflow for at least 30 minutes, then try charging again.
4. If the alert returns, leave it to dry for up to a day.

Don’t use a hairdryer or other heat source, don’t insert cotton buds or paper towels into the port, and don’t put the phone in rice. Wireless charging may still work in the meantime.

## Step 5: Restart the iPhone

A software glitch can stop charging from being recognised. Force restart the phone:

1. Press and quickly release the **volume up** button.
2. Press and quickly release the **volume down** button.
3. Press and hold the **side button** until the Apple logo appears, then release.

This works on iPhone 8 and later. Plug the phone back in and leave it for 30 minutes.

## Step 6: Check charging settings (for stopping at 80%)

If charging stops at or around 80%, check **Settings > Battery > Charging** (on some iOS versions this is under **Battery Health & Charging**):

- **Optimised Battery Charging** delays charging past 80% until closer to when you usually unplug. You’ll see a notification on the lock screen; tap and hold it to charge fully now.
- **Charging limit** options on newer models can cap charging at 80% or another level by design.

Heat also pauses charging. If the phone is warm — in direct sun, under a pillow, or while gaming — iPhone may show “Charging On Hold” until it cools down. Move it somewhere cooler and remove a thick case.

## Step 7: Update iOS

Go to **Settings > General > Software Update** and install any available update. Updates regularly fix battery and charging bugs, and some accessories need a current iOS version to work properly. You’ll need Wi-Fi — if that isn’t working either, see our guide to [fixing a Wi-Fi connection that isn’t working](/why-is-my-wifi-not-working).

## Step 8: Wireless charging problems

- Remove the case (especially thick, metal or wallet cases) and anything between the phone and the charger, such as cards.
- Centre the phone on the charging pad — for MagSafe, let the magnets align it.
- Make sure the pad itself is powered with its recommended adapter.

## Step 9: Check battery health

Go to **Settings > Battery > Battery Health** (or **Battery Health & Charging**). If the maximum capacity is low or you see a service message, the battery may need replacing. An ageing battery can also cause unexpected shutdowns and very fast draining.

## Step 10: Get it checked

If none of the above works, the charging port, battery or logic board may need repair. Contact Apple Support or an Apple Authorised Service Provider. Before you go, back up your iPhone if you can.

## Quick reference

| Symptom | Most likely cause | Try first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not charging at all | Cable, adapter or lint in the port | Different cable and adapter; clean the port |
| Charges only at an angle | Lint or worn cable | Clean the port; replace the cable |
| Stops at 80% | Optimised charging, charge limit, or heat | Check battery settings; let it cool |
| “Accessory may not be supported” | Uncertified or damaged cable, dirty port | Use a certified cable; clean the port |
| Liquid detected | Moisture in the port | Unplug and let it dry fully |
| Drains fast, shuts down | Worn battery | Check Battery Health |
