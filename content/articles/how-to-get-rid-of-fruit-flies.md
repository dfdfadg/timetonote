Fruit flies are tiny (about 3 mm), tan-coloured flies with red eyes that hover around fruit bowls, bins and sinks. A few can become a swarm quickly, because each female can lay hundreds of eggs and a new generation can be flying in about a week.

That’s why trapping alone rarely works. The fix has two parts: **remove what they’re breeding in** and **trap the adults** until the cycle is broken.

## Step 1: Find and remove where they’re breeding

Fruit flies lay eggs on moist, fermenting organic matter. Check all of these:

- **Fruit and vegetables** on the counter — especially bananas, tomatoes, onions and potatoes. Check the bottom of the bag or basket for anything soft or rotting.
- **Kitchen bin and food-waste caddy**, including residue stuck under the liner.
- **Recycling** — bottles, cans and jars with a little juice, wine, beer or sauce left in them.
- **Drains and garbage disposals**, where a film of food builds up.
- **Sponges, dishcloths and mops** left damp.
- **Spills** under appliances, behind the fridge, or in the bottom of cupboards.
- **Pet food bowls** left out.

Throw away overripe or damaged produce, take the rubbish out, rinse recycling, and wipe up any sticky residue.

> [!TIP]
> Not sure if a drain is the source? Put a strip of clear tape loosely over the drain opening overnight (sticky side down, leaving a small gap for air). If flies are stuck to it in the morning, they’re breeding in the drain.

## Step 2: Clean the drains

Scrub inside the drain opening and around the plug with a stiff brush and hot, soapy water to remove the film where larvae live. Flush with very hot water. If you have plastic (PVC) pipes, use hot tap water rather than boiling water, which can soften pipe joints over time. For waste disposals, run them with plenty of water and clean the rubber splash guard.

## Step 3: Set traps for the adults

Traps catch the flies already flying so they can’t lay more eggs. These work well:

### Apple cider vinegar trap (best DIY option)

1. Pour a few centimetres of apple cider vinegar into a jar or glass.
2. Add one or two drops of washing-up liquid — this breaks the surface tension so flies sink instead of landing and leaving.
3. Cover with cling film, secure it with a rubber band, and poke a few small holes in it with a toothpick.
4. Place it where you see the most flies. Replace every few days.

### Wine or beer trap

A small amount of leftover red wine or beer in a bottle works the same way — the narrow neck makes it hard for flies to escape.

### Fruit and paper-cone trap

Put a piece of very ripe fruit in a jar and roll a sheet of paper into a cone with a small opening at the tip. Place the cone in the jar mouth, tip down. Flies can find their way in but struggle to get out.

### Shop-bought traps

Sticky traps and ready-made fruit fly traps work too. Keep sticky traps away from food preparation areas.

## Step 4: Keep going for two weeks

Even after you remove breeding sites, eggs and larvae already present will keep hatching for a while. Keep the traps topped up and keep the kitchen clear of exposed food for at least one to two weeks — until you’ve seen no new flies for several days.

## How to stop fruit flies coming back

- Store ripe fruit in the fridge, or buy smaller amounts more often.
- Wash fruit and vegetables when you get home — eggs can arrive on produce.
- Empty food-waste bins every day or two in warm weather, and keep the lid closed.
- Rinse bottles and cans before putting them in recycling.
- Wring out and dry sponges and dishcloths, and replace them regularly.
- Wipe counters and clean drains weekly.

## Is it actually fruit flies?

Other small flies look similar but need a different fix:

| Fly | What it looks like | Where it breeds | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fruit fly | Tan body, red eyes | Ripe fruit, bins, spills | Remove food sources; vinegar trap |
| Drain fly | Fuzzy, moth-like wings | Slime inside drains | Scrub and clean drains thoroughly |
| Fungus gnat | Dark, thin, long legs | Damp soil of house plants | Let the soil dry out between watering; yellow sticky traps |

If flies keep appearing despite all of this, check for a hidden source such as a forgotten bag of potatoes, a spill under an appliance, or a damp problem in a cupboard.
