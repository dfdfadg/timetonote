A burning smell the first time you turn on the heat each fall is **usually normal**. Dust settles on the furnace or heater parts over the summer. When the heat comes on, that dust burns off and makes a **dusty, slightly burnt smell** that should fade within **a few minutes to a few hours**.

But some smells are a warning. **A smell like hot wires, melting plastic, or rotten eggs is not normal.** Here is how to tell the difference and what to do.

## Quick guide: what the smell means

| What it smells like | Likely cause | How serious | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dusty burning, first use of the season | Dust burning off | Normal | Open a window, wait a few hours |
| Burning dust that will not go away | Dirty filter or lots of dust inside | Low | Change the filter, call for a tune-up |
| Hot wires or melting plastic | Overheating motor or wiring | High | Turn the heat off and call a pro |
| Burning rubber | Worn belt or overheating motor | Medium to high | Turn the heat off and call a pro |
| Rotten eggs or sulfur | Natural gas leak | Emergency | Leave the house and call the gas company |
| Musty or moldy | Moisture in ducts or the filter | Low to medium | Change the filter, check for leaks |
| Smoky, like a fireplace | Blocked flue or chimney | High | Turn the heat off and call a pro |

## Why heat smells like burning the first time

During the warm months, dust floats through your home and lands on the parts of your heating system that get hot:

- **Gas furnaces:** the burners and heat exchanger
- **Electric furnaces and heat pumps:** the electric heat strips
- **Baseboard and space heaters:** the heating elements

The first time these parts heat up, the dust burns. Carrier, a major furnace maker, says this smell is a normal part of starting up the heat and should go away once the dust is burned off.

You may notice it again after a long break in the heating season, or the first time you use backup heat on a heat pump.

## How to get rid of the normal dust smell

1. **Open a window or two** to let fresh air in.
2. **Let the heat run** for an hour or two at a comfortable setting.
3. **Change the furnace filter** if it looks gray or dusty.
4. **Vacuum the vents and registers** to remove dust you can reach.

If the smell is gone by the end of the day, you have nothing to worry about.

## When the burning smell is a problem

Call an HVAC technician if:

- The smell **lasts more than a day**
- The smell gets **stronger** instead of weaker
- It smells like **hot metal, wires, or plastic**
- You see **smoke** coming from the furnace or vents
- The furnace makes **new noises**, like squealing, buzzing, or grinding
- The furnace **shuts off on its own** over and over

An electrical smell often comes from a part that is overheating, like the blower motor. Running it can damage the furnace and, in rare cases, start a fire.

## What to do if you smell something dangerous

### If you smell hot wires, plastic, or see smoke

1. **Turn the heat off** at the thermostat.
2. **Turn off power to the furnace.** Most furnaces have a switch that looks like a light switch on or near the unit.
3. **If you see flames or heavy smoke, leave the house and call 911.**
4. **Call an HVAC technician** before using the heat again.

### If you smell rotten eggs or gas

Natural gas has no smell on its own. Gas companies add a chemical that smells like rotten eggs so you can notice a leak.

1. **Leave the house right away.** Take people and pets with you.
2. **Do not touch light switches, appliances, or phones** inside. A spark can ignite gas.
3. **Do not light matches or lighters.**
4. **Call your gas company or 911 from outside** or from a neighbor's home.
5. **Do not go back inside** until they say it is safe.

## Protect your family from carbon monoxide

Gas, oil, and wood heat can make **carbon monoxide (CO)**, a gas you cannot see or smell. A cracked heat exchanger or a blocked flue can let it into your home.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends a **CO alarm on every level of your home and outside sleeping areas**. Test them when you turn on the heat for the season.

Signs of CO poisoning can feel like the flu: headache, dizziness, upset stomach, and feeling tired or confused. If your CO alarm goes off or several people feel sick at once, **get everyone outside to fresh air and call 911**.

If your alarms are chirping, our guide on [why a smoke detector chirps](/why-is-my-smoke-detector-chirping) explains what each sound means. Many combo smoke and CO alarms use the same signals.

## Other heaters that smell like burning

### Space heaters

New space heaters often smell for the first hour or two as coatings burn off. Dust on an older heater can smell too. But a space heater should **never** smell like melting plastic. If it does, unplug it.

Keep space heaters **3 feet away** from anything that can burn, like curtains, bedding, and furniture. Plug them straight into the wall, not into an extension cord or power strip. Curious what one costs to run? Try our [space heater cost calculator](/tools/space-heater-cost-calculator).

### Heat pumps

A heat pump may smell dusty the first time its backup heat strips turn on. That should fade the same way a furnace smell does. A strong electrical smell is a reason to turn it off and call a pro.

### Baseboard heaters

Dust, pet hair, and small items that fall behind baseboard heaters can burn. Vacuum along the fins before the cold season, and keep furniture and curtains away from them.

## How to prevent burning smells

- **Change your furnace filter** every 1 to 3 months during heating season.
- **Get a yearly tune-up** in early fall, before the first cold snap. A technician cleans parts and checks for safety problems like a cracked heat exchanger.
- **Vacuum vents and registers** a few times a year.
- **Keep the area around the furnace clear.** Do not store boxes, paint, or cleaning products next to it.
- **Turn the heat on for a test run** on a mild fall day. That way the dust burns off before you really need heat.

## Heat on, but the air is cold?

If the smell fades but your vents blow cool air, the problem may be the filter, the thermostat, or the pilot light. Our guide on a [furnace that blows cool air instead of warm](/why-is-my-furnace-blowing-cold-air) walks through the fixes.

## The bottom line

A dusty burning smell when you first turn on the heat is normal. It is just dust burning off, and it should fade within a few hours. Open a window, change the filter, and let the heat run. But if the smell lasts more than a day, smells like hot wires or plastic, or comes with smoke, turn the heat off and call an HVAC technician. If you smell rotten eggs, leave the house and call the gas company from outside.
