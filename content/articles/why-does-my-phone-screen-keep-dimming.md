A phone screen keeps dimming by itself most often because of **auto-brightness**, **power-saving modes**, **heat**, or **attention and screen timeout settings**. These features change the brightness on purpose to save battery or protect the phone. A case or screen protector covering the **light sensor** can also make the phone think the room is dark.

The good news is that most dimming is caused by a setting you can change. Here is how to find the cause and fix it on **iPhone** and **Android**.

## What kind of dimming do you see?

| What happens | Most likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| Brightness changes when you move to a darker or brighter place | Auto-brightness |
| Screen gets darker when the battery is low | Low Power Mode or power saving |
| Screen dims when the phone is hot, like in the sun or while gaming | Heat protection |
| Screen dims when you look away (iPhone with Face ID) | Attention Aware Features |
| Screen dims a few seconds before turning off | Screen timeout or auto-lock |
| Screen looks warmer or yellowish at night | Night Shift, Night Light, or True Tone (color, not brightness) |
| Screen flickers or dims randomly, even at full brightness | Possible hardware problem |

## How to stop screen dimming on iPhone

### 1. Turn off auto-brightness

Go to **Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size**, scroll down, and turn off **Auto-Brightness**. Then set the brightness you want in **Control Center**.

Apple recommends keeping auto-brightness on to save battery, so you may want to turn it back on later.

### 2. Turn off Low Power Mode

Low Power Mode lowers brightness and other features to save battery. Go to **Settings > Battery** and turn off **Low Power Mode**, or charge your phone. The battery icon turns yellow when Low Power Mode is on.

### 3. Turn off Attention Aware Features

On iPhones with Face ID, the screen can dim when you are not looking at it. Go to **Settings > Face ID & Passcode** and turn off **Attention Aware Features**.

### 4. Check True Tone and Night Shift

These change the **color** of the screen, which can make it look dimmer.

- **True Tone:** **Settings > Display & Brightness > True Tone**
- **Night Shift:** **Settings > Display & Brightness > Night Shift**

### 5. Check Reduce White Point

This setting makes bright colors less intense. Go to **Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size** and turn off **Reduce White Point**.

### 6. Change Auto-Lock

If the screen dims right before it locks, go to **Settings > Display & Brightness > Auto-Lock** and choose a longer time.

## How to stop screen dimming on Android

Menu names are a little different on each phone, but the settings are similar.

### 1. Turn off adaptive brightness

Go to **Settings > Display** and turn off **Adaptive brightness** (sometimes called **Auto brightness**).

### 2. Turn off power saving mode

Go to **Settings > Battery** (or **Battery and device care**) and turn off **Power saving** or **Battery saver**.

### 3. Turn off Extra dim

Some phones, including many Samsung and Pixel phones, have an **Extra dim** option in **Settings > Accessibility** or in Quick Settings. Make sure it is off.

### 4. Check screen timeout

Go to **Settings > Display > Screen timeout** and choose a longer time. Many phones dim the screen a few seconds before it turns off.

### 5. Check Eye comfort or Night Light

**Eye comfort shield** (Samsung) or **Night Light** (Pixel) make the screen warmer and can look dimmer. Find them in **Settings > Display**.

## Why your phone dims when it gets hot

Your screen is one of the biggest sources of heat in your phone. When the phone gets too warm, it lowers the brightness to cool down. Apple says iPhones may dim the display when they get too hot, and they work best when the air around them is between 32° and 95°F.

**Fix:**

- Move the phone out of direct sunlight.
- Take off a thick case.
- Stop gaming or streaming for a while.
- Stop charging until it cools.

Brightness should return once the phone cools. Our guide on [why phones overheat](/why-does-my-phone-get-hot) explains the common causes.

## Check the light sensor

Your phone has a **light sensor** near the top of the screen, usually near the front camera. If something covers it, the phone thinks it is dark and lowers the brightness.

**Fix:**

- Clean the top of the screen.
- Make sure your **screen protector** does not cover the sensor area.
- Check that your **case** does not block the top edge.

## Update and restart your phone

Software bugs can cause brightness problems. Restart your phone, then install any updates:

- **iPhone:** **Settings > General > Software Update**
- **Android:** **Settings > System > Software update**

## When dimming is a hardware problem

If the screen dims or flickers even after you turn off all these features, and it is not hot, the display or its parts may be damaged. This is more likely after a drop or water damage. If your phone also shuts off or restarts on its own, see our guide on [a phone that keeps restarting](/why-does-my-phone-keep-restarting).

Contact the phone maker or a trusted repair shop, especially if the phone is under warranty.

## Why phones dim on purpose

It helps to know why these features exist. The screen uses more battery than almost anything else on your phone. Auto-brightness, Low Power Mode, and screen timeout all save battery by keeping the screen only as bright as it needs to be. Heat protection keeps the battery and parts from damage. So instead of turning everything off, many people keep auto-brightness on and just turn off the one feature that bothers them.

## In short

Most screen dimming comes from auto-brightness, power saving, heat, or attention and timeout settings. Turn off the feature causing it, keep the light sensor clear, and let a hot phone cool down. If the screen still dims or flickers after that, have it checked for a hardware problem.
