When the power goes out, **stay safe first: use flashlights instead of candles, keep your fridge and freezer closed, unplug electronics, and report the outage to your electric company.** Never run a generator or grill indoors. The FDA says food in a closed fridge stays safe for about **4 hours**, and a full freezer for about **48 hours**.

Most outages end within a few hours. But winter storms, hurricanes, and heat waves can cause longer ones, so it helps to know what to do and to be ready ahead of time.

## What to do right away

1. **Check if it is just your home.** Look outside to see if neighbors have power. If only your home is dark, check your circuit breaker panel for a tripped breaker.
2. **Report the outage** to your electric company by phone, app, or website.
3. **Use flashlights,** not candles, to lower the risk of fire.
4. **Unplug electronics** like TVs and computers to protect them from a power surge when power returns.
5. **Leave one light switched on** so you know when the power is back.
6. **Keep the fridge and freezer closed.**
7. **Stay away from downed power lines.** Always assume they are live, and call 911 or your utility.

## Keep food safe

| Appliance | Food stays safe for about | If doors stay closed |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerator | 4 hours | Yes |
| Full freezer | 48 hours | Yes |
| Half-full freezer | 24 hours | Yes |

After the power comes back:

- **Check temperatures.** The fridge should be at 40°F or colder, and the freezer at 0°F or colder. An appliance thermometer helps.
- **Throw out perishable food** like meat, milk, eggs, and leftovers that were above 40°F for 2 hours or more.
- **Frozen food with ice crystals** or at 40°F or below can usually be refrozen.
- **When in doubt, throw it out.** Never taste food to check if it is safe.

If your fridge acts strangely after the outage, see our guide on [a fridge that is not cooling but the freezer is](/fridge-not-cooling-but-freezer-is).

## Stay warm or cool safely

**In cold weather:**

- Close off unused rooms and gather in one room.
- Wear layers and use blankets.
- Let faucets drip slightly to help keep pipes from freezing.
- **Never use a gas stove, oven, or grill to heat your home.**

**In hot weather:**

- Stay on the lowest floor and keep blinds closed.
- Drink plenty of water.
- Go to a cooling center or a friend's home with power if it gets too hot, especially for older adults, babies, and people with health problems.

## Use a generator safely

Ready.gov and the CDC warn that generators make **carbon monoxide (CO)**, a gas you cannot see or smell that can kill quickly.

- **Only run generators outdoors,** at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents.
- **Never** use one in a home, garage, basement, or shed, even with the door open.
- **Plug appliances straight into the generator** with heavy-duty outdoor cords.
- **Never plug a generator into a wall outlet.** This "backfeeding" can hurt utility workers and neighbors. Use a transfer switch installed by an electrician.
- **Have working CO alarms** with fresh batteries. If one chirps, see our guide on [chirping smoke and carbon monoxide alarms](/why-is-my-smoke-detector-chirping).

## When the power comes back

1. **Plug appliances back in slowly,** one at a time, to avoid a surge.
2. **Check your food** using the guide above.
3. **Reset clocks and devices.**
4. **Check your heating.** If the furnace does not restart, our guide on [a furnace blowing cold air](/why-is-my-furnace-blowing-cold-air) can help.
5. **Restock your emergency kit.**

## Build a power outage kit

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Flashlights and extra batteries | Safe light |
| Battery or crank radio | News and weather updates |
| Phone power bank | Keep your phone charged |
| Bottled water (1 gallon per person per day) | Drinking and basic needs |
| Non-perishable food and a manual can opener | Meals without cooking |
| First aid kit and medicines | Health needs |
| Blankets and warm clothes | Cold weather |
| Cooler and ice packs | Keep medicine and food cold |
| Appliance thermometers | Check food safety |

## Special tips

- **Medical devices:** If someone uses powered medical equipment, talk to your doctor and utility about a backup plan. Some utilities keep a priority list.
- **Garage doors:** Learn how to open yours by hand with the release cord.
- **Sump pumps:** A battery backup can keep a basement from flooding during storms. Flooding can also lead to [hidden water damage](/hidden-water-leaks).
- **Check on neighbors,** especially older adults and people who live alone.

## The bottom line

In a power outage, stay safe with flashlights, keep the fridge and freezer closed, unplug electronics, and report it to your utility. Food lasts about 4 hours in a closed fridge and 24 to 48 hours in a freezer. Never run a generator, grill, or gas stove indoors. Build a simple kit now so you are ready for the next outage.
