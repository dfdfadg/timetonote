**A big drop in battery life in the first few days after an iOS update is common.** Your iPhone is busy in the background, sorting photos, rebuilding search, and updating apps. It usually settles in 1 to 3 days. If it does not, update your apps, restart your phone, check which apps use the most battery, and turn off a few power-hungry settings.

You updated to iOS 27, and now your battery is gone by lunch. It dropped from 80 percent to 20 percent while the phone sat on the table. That feels wrong.

You are not alone. Many people notice faster drain right after a big update. In most cases it is a short-term problem, and you can help it along. This guide explains what is going on, how long to wait, and ten easy fixes. We use simple words, so anyone can follow along.

## Why does iOS 27 drain the battery at first?

A big iPhone update does more than change how the screen looks. After it installs, your phone does a lot of hidden work. Here are the main jobs it does:

- **Rebuilds the search index.** This is how your phone finds your files, texts, and photos fast.
- **Scans your photos.** Your phone sorts faces, places, and objects again.
- **Updates your apps.** Many apps update in the background to work with the new system.
- **Downloads extra files.** New features may need extra data.
- **Gets new smart features ready.** New AI tools can need more work to set up.

All of this takes power. It is like moving into a new house. For the first few days, you are busy unpacking. Once the boxes are gone, things calm down.

This extra work does not harm your battery. Most people see normal battery life again after a few days.

## How long should the drain last?

Here is a simple guide:

| Time since the update | What is normal |
|---|---|
| Day 1 to 2 | Faster drain, and the phone may feel warm |
| Day 3 to 4 | Battery should start to improve |
| Day 5 to 7 | Battery life should be back to normal |
| After 1 week | Not normal. Time to dig deeper |

If your battery is still draining fast after about a week, the update is probably not the cause anymore. The reason may be an app, a setting, or an old battery.

## Is your phone also hot?

Fast drain and a hot phone often go together. Both can come from the same busy work in the background. If your phone feels hot as well, read why a [phone gets hot](/why-does-my-phone-get-hot). Never charge or use a phone that feels very hot, and take it out of a case until it cools down.

## Fix 1: Wait and plug it in overnight

This is the first step, and it is the best one for the first few days. Leave your iPhone plugged in overnight on Wi-Fi. This gives it time to finish its background jobs while you sleep.

Tips for the best result:

- Use the charger that came with your phone, or a good-quality one.
- Keep the phone on Wi-Fi.
- Do not use it while it works.
- Repeat for two or three nights.

If your phone has trouble charging at all, our guide on [why an iPhone is not charging](/iphone-not-charging) can help.

## Fix 2: Restart your iPhone

A restart clears out small glitches. It is quick, and it can fix a lot.

1. Press and hold the side button and a volume button together.
2. Slide to power off.
3. Wait 30 seconds.
4. Press and hold the side button to turn it back on.

Do this once after the update, and again if the battery still drains the next day. Some phones also keep restarting on their own. If that is your problem, read why a [phone keeps restarting](/why-does-my-phone-keep-restarting).

## Fix 3: Update your apps

Old apps can act badly after a new system update. They may use more power than they should. Updating fixes this.

1. Open the App Store.
2. Tap your profile picture at the top.
3. Scroll down to see waiting updates.
4. Tap "Update All."

Do this over Wi-Fi. If you have not yet installed the update itself, our guide on [how to update to iOS 27 on iPhone](/how-to-update-to-ios-27-on-iphone) walks you through it. Also, if the newest small update is out, install that too. Apple often fixes bugs in small updates.

## Fix 4: Find out which apps use the most battery

Your iPhone can show you exactly which apps are eating the battery.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Battery**.
3. Look at the list of apps and the percent each one used.
4. Switch between "Last 24 Hours" and "Last 10 Days."

Look for an app with a very high number, especially one you did not use much. Social apps, video apps, and maps are common. If one app is the problem, try these:

- Update it.
- Close it and reopen it.
- Delete it and install it again.
- Look for a smaller, lighter app.

Some apps, like Facebook or Instagram, are known to use a lot of power. Check the list for apps you did not expect.

## Fix 5: Turn off Background App Refresh for apps you do not need

Background App Refresh lets apps update when you are not using them. That uses power.

1. Go to **Settings**.
2. Tap **General**.
3. Tap **Background App Refresh**.
4. Turn it off for apps that do not need it, or choose "Wi-Fi" only.

Keep it on for the apps you care about, like maps or messages. Turn it off for games, shopping apps, and anything you rarely open.

## Fix 6: Lower your screen brightness and use auto-brightness

Your screen is one of the biggest power users. A bright screen drains the battery fast.

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Accessibility**.
3. Tap **Display and Text Size**.
4. Turn on **Auto-Brightness**.

You can also pull down Control Center and drag the brightness bar lower. If your screen keeps getting dim on its own, see why a [phone screen keeps dimming](/why-does-my-phone-screen-keep-dimming). That may be a power-saving setting at work.

Dark Mode can also help on many newer iPhones.

## Fix 7: Turn on Low Power Mode

Low Power Mode cuts back on background work and some visual effects. It is a fast way to make your battery last longer on a busy day.

1. Go to **Settings**.
2. Tap **Battery**.
3. Turn on **Low Power Mode**.

You can also add it to Control Center. The battery icon turns yellow when it is on. Your phone may feel a bit slower, but it saves power. Most people use this as a short-term tool while the phone settles down after the update.

## Fix 8: Check location, Bluetooth, and Wi-Fi settings

Some settings keep working even when you do not need them.

**Location Services:**

1. Go to **Settings**, then **Privacy and Security**, then **Location Services**.
2. Look at each app. Change "Always" to "While Using" for most apps.
3. Turn it off for apps that do not need your location.

**Bluetooth:** If your phone keeps trying to connect and failing, it uses extra power. Our guide on [why Bluetooth keeps disconnecting](/why-does-my-bluetooth-keep-disconnecting) shows how to fix it.

**Wi-Fi:** A weak signal makes your phone work harder. If your Wi-Fi is slow, try our tips for [a slow internet connection](/why-is-my-internet-so-slow) and [Wi-Fi that keeps failing](/why-is-my-wifi-not-working).

## Fix 9: Free up storage

A nearly full phone has to work harder. It also has less room for the files it needs after an update.

Check your storage:

1. Go to **Settings**, then **General**, then **iPhone Storage**.
2. Look at how much space is left.
3. Delete old photos, videos, and apps you do not use.

Try to keep at least 10 percent of your space free. See our guide on [why your phone storage is full](/why-is-my-phone-storage-full) for easy ways to clear space.

## Fix 10: Check your battery health

Sometimes the update is not the real problem. Your battery may simply be old. Batteries wear out over time, and a weak battery drains fast no matter what software you use.

Check it:

1. Go to **Settings**.
2. Tap **Battery**.
3. Tap **Battery Health** (on some models it is called **Battery Health & Charging**).
4. Look at "Maximum Capacity."

Here is a quick guide:

| Maximum capacity | What it means |
|---|---|
| 90 percent and up | Healthy |
| 80 to 89 percent | Normal wear |
| Below 80 percent | Weak. Consider a battery replacement |

Apple says a normal iPhone battery is designed to keep up to 80 percent of its original capacity after a set number of full charge cycles under normal conditions. The Battery Health screen will also tell you if the battery is significantly degraded.

If your battery is below 80 percent, a battery replacement may do more than any setting. Apple stores and trusted repair shops can do this. Ask about the price first.

## Things that waste battery that you might not think about

A few small habits drain more power than you would guess.

- **Many open apps.** Closing apps will not always help, but a stuck app can keep running. Close any app that is acting strange.
- **Live widgets.** Widgets that update often, like weather and news, use power.
- **Always-on display.** If your phone has it, turning it off can help a lot.
- **Push email.** Switching email to "Fetch" every 15 minutes uses less power.
- **Cold weather.** Cold air makes batteries drain faster. Keep your phone in a pocket, not outside.
- **Poor cell signal.** Weak service makes your phone search harder and use more power.
- **Very bright games and videos.** These are heavy on the screen and the processor.

## What not to do

Some tips online do not help, and some can cause trouble.

- **Do not do a full factory reset right away.** It will not help in most cases. The same background jobs start again after you set up the phone.
- **Do not use fake "battery saver" apps.** They often use more power than they save.
- **Do not leave your phone in a hot car.** Heat is bad for batteries.
- **Do not use cheap, no-name chargers.** They can hurt your battery.

## Should you go back to the old version of iOS?

Most of the time, no. The drain is short-term. Going back is hard, it may not even be allowed after Apple stops signing old versions, and you risk losing data. Wait a week, try the fixes above, and then decide. If you still want help, talk to Apple Support.

## When to get help

Contact Apple Support or a repair shop if:

- Battery health is below 80 percent.
- The phone is swollen, or the screen is lifting. Stop using it right away.
- The phone gets very hot while sitting idle.
- Your battery drops by half in an hour.
- The drain continues after a week and nothing helps.

## The bottom line

If your iPhone battery drains fast after the iOS 27 update, give it a few days. Plug it in overnight, restart it, and update your apps. If the problem sticks around, find the apps that use the most power, adjust a few settings, and check your battery health.

Most people see their battery life return to normal within a week. And if yours does not, you now have a clear plan and know when to ask for help.
