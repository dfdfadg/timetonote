Cleaning a dusty house takes less time when you do it in the right order and with the right tools. The two rules that matter most are simple: **work from top to bottom** and **trap dust rather than move it**.

Below is the method, room by room, followed by a routine that keeps dust from building up again. If dust returns unusually fast, it’s worth also reading about [the common causes of household dust](/why-is-my-house-so-dusty) — cleaning alone won’t fix a clogged filter or a draughty window.

## What you need

- **Microfibre cloths** (several). Slightly damp microfibre grabs fine dust instead of pushing it into the air.
- **A vacuum cleaner with a HEPA filter** and its brush and crevice attachments.
- **An extendable duster** with a washable microfibre head for ceilings, fans and high shelves.
- **A spray bottle** with water and a drop of washing-up liquid for greasy dust in kitchens.
- **An old pillowcase** for ceiling-fan blades.
- **A mop** suitable for your hard floors.

Skip feather dusters and dry cloths. They make a room *look* clean for an hour, then the dust settles back down.

## The method: top to bottom, dry to wet

### Step 1: Prepare the room

Open a window if the weather allows, and switch on an air purifier if you have one. Put away clutter from surfaces so you can wipe them in one pass. Remove cushion covers, throws and any washable fabrics for the laundry.

### Step 2: Start at the ceiling

Use the extendable duster on ceiling corners, cobwebs, light fittings and the top of door frames. For ceiling fans, slide an old pillowcase over each blade and pull it back — the dust stays inside the pillowcase instead of raining onto the room.

### Step 3: Work down through high surfaces

Wipe the tops of wardrobes, bookcases, curtain rails, picture frames and air vents. Fold your microfibre cloth into quarters and turn to a clean side as each one gets dirty.

### Step 4: Blinds, curtains and windowsills

- **Venetian blinds:** close the slats, wipe with a damp cloth, then turn them the other way and repeat.
- **Curtains:** vacuum with the brush attachment from top to bottom, or wash them if the care label allows.
- **Windowsills and tracks:** vacuum loose grit with the crevice tool, then wipe.

### Step 5: Furniture and electronics

Wipe tables, shelves and ornaments with a lightly damp cloth. For TVs, monitors and electronics, switch them off and use a *dry*, clean microfibre cloth — never spray liquid directly onto a screen. Vacuum upholstered furniture with the brush attachment, including under the cushions.

### Step 6: Skirting boards, doors and radiators

Wipe skirting boards (baseboards), door panels and handles. Radiators trap a surprising amount of dust: slide a long, thin radiator brush between the panels, then vacuum what falls out.

### Step 7: Vacuum everything

Vacuum last so you collect everything that fell during the earlier steps. Go slowly — a slow pass picks up far more from carpets than several fast ones. Work from the far side of the room towards the door, and don’t forget under beds and furniture where dust collects undisturbed.

### Step 8: Mop hard floors

Finish with a damp mop on hard floors to lift the fine layer the vacuum leaves behind.

> [!TIP]
> Empty the vacuum outdoors, and wash microfibre cloths without fabric softener — softener coats the fibres and stops them trapping dust.

## Room-by-room tips

| Room | Focus on | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bedroom | Bedding, under the bed, tops of wardrobes | Beds shed fibres and skin; hidden spaces go untouched for weeks |
| Living room | Sofa, rugs, TV unit, blinds | High traffic and lots of fabric |
| Kitchen | Top of cupboards, extractor hood, fridge top | Dust mixes with cooking grease and sticks |
| Home office | Keyboard, monitor, computer vents | Electronics attract dust with static |
| Hallway | Mats, skirting boards, shoe storage | Where outdoor dirt enters the home |

For grease-coated dust on kitchen cupboards, a cloth dampened with warm water and a little washing-up liquid works better than plain water. Dry the surface afterwards.

## A simple routine to keep dust down

A little regularly beats a big clean occasionally:

- **Every day (5 minutes):** shake out or vacuum entrance mats; quick wipe of the most-used surfaces.
- **Twice a week:** vacuum carpets and rugs in busy rooms.
- **Weekly:** damp-dust all rooms, vacuum everywhere, mop hard floors, wash bedding.
- **Monthly:** check your heating/air conditioning filter, dust ceiling fans and light fittings, vacuum curtains and upholstery.
- **Every few months:** move furniture and clean underneath, wash curtains and cushion covers.

If you’re doing all of this and dust still piles up, the problem is probably coming in faster than you can remove it. Go back to our guide on [why a house gets dusty so quickly](/why-is-my-house-so-dusty) to track down the source.

## Cleaning safely if you have allergies

If dust makes you sneeze or wheeze, wear a well-fitting disposable mask (FFP2/N95 type) while dusting and vacuuming, keep the window open, and ideally leave the room for a while afterwards so airborne particles can settle or be filtered out. If symptoms are persistent, speak to a doctor or pharmacist.
