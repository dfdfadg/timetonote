To paint a room like a pro, **prep the walls first, cut in the edges with a brush, then roll the walls in small sections while the paint is still wet.** Most rooms need two coats. Good prep and a steady routine matter more than expensive tools, and even a first-timer can get a clean, even finish in a weekend.

This guide walks you through each step, from buying the right amount of paint to cleaning your brushes.

**Older home?** Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. The EPA advises testing before you sand or scrape old paint, because the dust can be harmful, especially for kids and pregnant women.

## Supplies you need

- Paint and primer (if needed)
- An angled 2 to 2.5 inch brush for cutting in
- A 9-inch roller frame and roller covers
- A paint tray and liners
- An extension pole for the roller
- Painter's tape
- Drop cloths or old sheets
- Spackle and a putty knife
- Fine sandpaper or a sanding sponge
- A damp cloth or sponge
- A step stool or small ladder

**Roller nap tip:** Use a 3/8 inch nap for smooth or lightly textured walls, and a 1/2 inch nap for textured walls.

## How much paint to buy

One gallon usually covers about **350 to 400 square feet** with one coat, based on typical interior paints from brands like Sherwin-Williams.

1. Add up the width of all walls.
2. Multiply by the ceiling height to get the wall area.
3. Subtract about 20 square feet for each door and 15 for each window.
4. Divide by 350, then multiply by the number of coats.

| Room size (8 ft ceiling) | Wall area | Paint for 2 coats |
|---|---|---|
| 10 × 10 ft | About 320 sq ft | About 2 gallons |
| 12 × 12 ft | About 384 sq ft | About 2 to 2.5 gallons |
| 12 × 16 ft | About 448 sq ft | About 2.5 to 3 gallons |
| 15 × 20 ft | About 560 sq ft | About 3 to 3.5 gallons |

Buy a little extra for touch-ups later, and write the color name and code on the lid.

## Picking the right finish

| Finish | Look | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Flat or matte | No shine, hides flaws | Ceilings, low-traffic rooms |
| Eggshell | Soft glow, easy to wipe | Living rooms, bedrooms |
| Satin | Light shine, washable | Kids' rooms, hallways |
| Semi-gloss | Shiny, very washable | Trim, doors, bathrooms, kitchens |

## Step 1: Prep the room

1. **Move furniture** to the middle of the room and cover it.
2. **Lay drop cloths** along the walls.
3. **Remove outlet covers, switch plates, and wall hooks.** Keep screws in a bag.
4. **Fill holes and cracks** with spackle. Let it dry, then sand smooth. Small, straight cracks are common. If you see wide or growing cracks, read our guide on [when to worry about wall cracks](/wall-cracks-worry) before you paint over them.
5. **Clean the walls.** Wipe off dust and grease with a damp cloth. Paint sticks much better to clean walls. Our guide on [a simple house cleaning routine](/how-to-clean-a-dusty-house) has tips for dusty rooms.
6. **Tape the trim** if you do not have a steady hand. Press the edge down firmly so paint does not bleed under.

## Step 2: Prime if needed

Use primer on new drywall, patched spots, water stains, glossy paint, or when going from a dark color to a light one. Let it dry fully before painting.

If you see mold or mildew, clean it first and fix the moisture problem. Painting over it will not stop it from coming back. Our guide on [stopping condensation on windows](/why-is-there-condensation-on-my-windows) explains common moisture causes.

## Step 3: Cut in the edges

"Cutting in" means painting a neat strip along the ceiling, corners, and trim with a brush.

1. Dip the brush about one-third of the way into the paint and tap off the extra.
2. Paint a 2 to 3 inch strip along the edges, using the angled tip for a clean line.
3. Work on one wall at a time so the edges are still wet when you roll.

## Step 4: Roll the walls

1. Pour paint into the tray and roll the roller back and forth until it is evenly coated, not dripping.
2. Start about a foot from a corner and roll a large **W or M shape**, about 3 feet wide.
3. Fill in the shape with straight, up-and-down strokes without lifting the roller.
4. Move to the next section, overlapping the wet edge slightly.
5. Finish each wall with light top-to-bottom strokes for an even look.

**Keep a wet edge.** Always roll back into paint that is still wet. This is the secret to avoiding lap marks and streaks.

## Step 5: Add the second coat

Wait for the first coat to dry, usually 2 to 4 hours for latex paint. Then repeat the cutting in and rolling. The second coat gives even color and a stronger finish.

## Step 6: Clean up

- **Remove tape** while the last coat is still slightly wet, pulling at an angle, for the cleanest line.
- **Wash brushes and rollers** in warm, soapy water for latex paint.
- **Store leftover paint** with the lid tapped on tight, upside down, somewhere that does not freeze.
- **Put back outlet covers** once the paint is dry.
- **Keep the room ventilated** with windows open or a fan running.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Skipping prep.** Most bad paint jobs come from dirty walls and unfilled holes.
- **Too much paint on the roller.** It causes drips and runs.
- **Painting in poor light.** Missed spots show up later.
- **Stopping mid-wall.** It leaves visible lines where the paint dried.
- **Rushing the second coat.** Wet paint can peel or look blotchy.

## What order should you paint a room?

Pros work from the top down so drips do not land on finished work:

1. **Ceiling first.** Drips and splatters will fall on walls you have not painted yet.
2. **Walls next.** Cut in, then roll.
3. **Trim, doors, and baseboards last.** It is easier to cut a neat line on trim than on a wall.

## How long does it take to paint a room?

For one person doing a typical 12 by 12 foot bedroom with two coats, plan on most of a day:

| Task | Rough time |
|---|---|
| Move furniture, clean, patch, and tape | 1 to 2 hours |
| Cut in and roll the first coat | 2 to 3 hours |
| Drying time between coats | 2 to 4 hours |
| Second coat | 1.5 to 2 hours |
| Clean up | 30 minutes |

A small room or touch-up job goes faster. Ceilings, dark color changes, and lots of trim add time.

## Best temperature and humidity for painting

Paint dries best in mild, dry air. Check the label on your can, since some paints need it warmer. In general:

- Keep the room at **50°F or warmer** while you paint and while the paint dries. Avoid very hot rooms too, since paint that dries too fast can show lap marks.
- Lower humidity is better. High humidity makes paint dry slower and can leave streaks.
- Open a window or run a fan for fresh air, but do not point the fan right at wet paint.

## When can you hang pictures again?

Paint is **dry to the touch** in a few hours, but it takes much longer to fully **cure** and get hard.

- **Latex paint:** wait at least **24 hours** before hanging pictures on nails.
- **Oil-based paint:** wait at least **48 hours**.
- **Sticky hooks and strips:** wait longer. Many adhesive hook makers say to wait about **7 days** after painting, or the strips may peel the paint.
- **Pushing furniture against walls:** wait a few days, or the paint may stick and peel.

## The bottom line

Painting a room like a pro comes down to good prep and a steady routine. Clean and patch the walls, prime where needed, cut in the edges, and roll in small sections while keeping a wet edge. Plan on two coats and about one gallon for every 350 to 400 square feet per coat. Take your time, and you will get a smooth, even finish you can be proud of.
