If your house is still humid with the AC on, the most common reasons are **an AC that is too big and shuts off too fast, the thermostat fan set to ON, a dirty filter or coil, or too much moisture coming in from outside or from daily life.** Your AC cools the air and also pulls water out of it. When something stops it from doing the second job well, your home feels cool but damp and sticky.

The EPA suggests keeping indoor humidity **below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50 percent.** A small humidity meter from a hardware store tells you where you stand.

## Signs your home is too humid

- The air feels sticky or clammy even when it is cool
- Windows fog up on the inside
- A musty smell in closets, basements, or bathrooms
- Mold spots on walls, ceilings, or window frames
- Wood doors swelling and sticking
- A humidity meter reading above 60 percent

## 8 reasons your house is humid with the AC on

### 1. Your AC is too big for your home

This surprises many people. A bigger AC is not always better. An oversized unit cools the air very fast and shuts off before it runs long enough to pull out much moisture. This is called **short cycling**. The house gets cold but stays clammy.

**Fix:** An HVAC pro can check if your system is the right size. If it is oversized, a dehumidifier or a system with variable speed can help.

### 2. The fan is set to ON

If your thermostat fan is set to **ON**, the blower runs all the time. After the AC turns off, the fan keeps blowing air over the wet cooling coil, and some of that water goes back into your home.

**Fix:** Set the fan to **AUTO** so it only runs while the AC is cooling.

### 3. The air filter is dirty

A clogged filter cuts airflow. The coil can get too cold or even freeze, and the system cannot remove moisture well.

**Fix:** Check the filter every month in summer and change it at least every 3 months.

### 4. The coil or drain line is dirty

The indoor coil collects moisture from the air, and the water drains away through a small pipe. If the coil is dirty or the drain line is clogged, water can sit in the system and go back into the air.

**Fix:** Have your AC cleaned and checked by a pro once a year. They can clean the coil and clear the drain line.

### 5. Your AC is low on refrigerant or has another problem

Low refrigerant or a failing part can make the AC run without removing much moisture. You may also notice weak cooling or ice on the lines.

**Fix:** Call an HVAC technician. Refrigerant work requires a licensed pro.

### 6. Humid air is leaking in

Gaps around windows, doors, and attic hatches let warm, wet outside air into your home. Leaky ductwork in a hot attic or crawl space can pull damp air in too.

**Fix:** Seal gaps with caulk and weatherstripping. Ask an HVAC pro to check and seal leaky ducts.

### 7. Daily activities add moisture

Showers, cooking, drying clothes, and even breathing add water to the air. In a closed-up house with the AC on, it builds up.

**Fix:**

- Run bathroom fans during showers and for about 20 minutes after.
- Use the kitchen range hood when cooking.
- Make sure your dryer vents outside.
- Cover pots when cooking.

### 8. A damp basement or crawl space

Basements and crawl spaces are often the most humid parts of a home. That moisture can rise into the rest of the house. Hidden leaks can add to the problem. See our guide on [warning signs of hidden water leaks](/hidden-water-leaks).

**Fix:** Run a dehumidifier in the basement. Fix drainage problems outside, and make sure gutters send water away from the house.

## Quick fixes to lower humidity today

| Fix | Cost | How much it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Set the fan to AUTO | Free | Often a big difference |
| Change the air filter | $10 to $40 | Improves airflow and drying |
| Run bath and kitchen fans | Free | Removes moisture at the source |
| Use a dehumidifier | $150 to $350 | Directly removes water from the air |
| Seal gaps and leaks | $20 to $100 | Keeps humid air out |
| Annual AC tune-up | $75 to $200 | Keeps coil and drain working |

## Should you get a dehumidifier?

If your humidity stays above 50 to 60 percent after the fixes above, a dehumidifier is often the best next step.

- **Portable dehumidifiers** work well for one room or a basement. Look for an ENERGY STAR model sized for your space.
- **Whole-house dehumidifiers** connect to your HVAC system and treat every room. An HVAC pro installs them.

A drier home feels cooler, so you may be able to raise the thermostat a couple of degrees and still be comfortable. That can also lower your energy bill.

## Why humidity matters

High humidity is more than uncomfortable. It can lead to mold, dust mites, musty smells, peeling paint, and warped wood. If your home already smells damp, our guide on [getting rid of a musty room smell](/why-does-my-room-smell-musty) can help. In cooler months, too much moisture also shows up as [condensation on windows](/why-is-there-condensation-on-my-windows).

## When to call an HVAC pro

- The AC runs but the house stays above 60 percent humidity
- You see ice on the refrigerant lines or indoor coil
- Water is leaking from the indoor unit
- The AC turns on and off every few minutes
- Your system is more than 15 years old and struggling

## Should you turn the AC off when you leave?

It is tempting to turn the AC off to save money when you are at work or on a trip. But your AC also removes moisture. With it off on a hot, muggy day, indoor humidity can climb above 60 percent, which helps mold and dust mites grow.

A better plan:

- **Raise the thermostat 7 to 10 degrees** when you are gone for the day, instead of turning it off.
- **For longer trips,** set it to around 80 to 85°F, so it still runs now and then and pulls water out of the air.
- **Use a smart thermostat** to cool the house back down before you get home.

## Does AC "dry mode" help?

Many mini-split and window units have a **Dry** mode, often shown as a water drop icon. In this mode, the unit runs the fan slowly so the air stays on the cold coil longer. More water condenses and drains away, and the room cools only a little.

Dry mode is helpful on mild, damp days when it is not very hot but the air feels sticky. On very hot days, regular cooling mode with the fan on AUTO usually works better.

## Do ceiling fans lower humidity?

No. Ceiling fans move air, which helps sweat dry off your skin, so you **feel** cooler. But they do not remove water from the air. They can still help, because you may be comfortable with the thermostat a few degrees higher. Turn them off when no one is in the room.

## The bottom line

A house that stays humid with the AC on usually has an AC that short cycles, a fan set to ON, a dirty filter or coil, or too much moisture getting in. Start with the free fixes: set the fan to AUTO, change the filter, and run exhaust fans. Keep humidity between 30 and 50 percent with a dehumidifier if needed, and call an HVAC pro if the problem does not go away.
