In the United States, **the clocks go back one hour on Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 a.m. local time.** That is when daylight saving time ends, so 2:00 a.m. becomes 1:00 a.m. and you get an **extra hour of sleep**. Clocks go forward again on **Sunday, March 14, 2027**.

The easy way to remember it: **"Spring forward, fall back."**

## Daylight saving time dates

| Year | Clocks go forward (DST starts) | Clocks go back (DST ends) |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | Sunday, March 8 | **Sunday, November 1** |
| 2027 | Sunday, March 14 | Sunday, November 7 |
| 2028 | Sunday, March 12 | Sunday, November 5 |

All changes happen at **2:00 a.m. local time**.

## The rule behind the dates

According to NIST, daylight saving time in the U.S. **starts on the second Sunday in March** and **ends on the first Sunday in November**. This rule has been in place since 2007. Daylight saving time now lasts about 238 days, or roughly 65 percent of the year.

## What happens when clocks fall back

- At 2:00 a.m. on November 1, clocks go back to 1:00 a.m.
- The hour from 1:00 to 2:00 a.m. happens **twice** that night.
- Mornings get lighter sooner, but it gets **dark earlier in the evening**, often before 5:00 p.m. in many places.

## Which devices change on their own

| Device | Changes on its own? |
|---|---|
| Smartphones and tablets | Yes, with automatic time on |
| Computers | Yes, with automatic time on |
| Smart TVs, smart speakers | Usually yes |
| Cable boxes and many thermostats | Usually yes |
| Microwave and oven clocks | No |
| Car clocks | Often no, check your car |
| Wall clocks and watches | No |

If your phone did not change, go to **Settings** and turn on **automatic date and time**.

## Places that do not change their clocks

Not everyone in the U.S. changes their clocks:

- **Hawaii** stays on standard time all year.
- **Most of Arizona** does not change. The **Navajo Nation** in Arizona does.
- **U.S. territories** like Puerto Rico, Guam, the U.S. Virgin Islands, American Samoa, and the Northern Mariana Islands stay on standard time.

## What about other countries?

Many countries do not use daylight saving time at all. Others use different dates:

- **The UK and European Union** change clocks on the **last Sunday in October**, which is **October 25, 2026**. They move forward again on the last Sunday in March.
- **Canada** mostly follows the same dates as the U.S.
- **Mexico** ended daylight saving time for most of the country in 2022.

If you have family or meetings in other countries, the time difference may change by an hour for a week or two between their change and ours.

## A good time for a home safety check

Fire safety groups have long suggested using the time change as a reminder to check your smoke alarms. When you set your clocks back:

- **Test your smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.**
- **Change batteries** in alarms that have replaceable batteries.
- **Check the date** on the back of each alarm. Replace any that are 10 years old.

## Tips to adjust to the time change

Falling back is easier than springing forward, but it can still throw off your sleep.

- **Shift bedtime slowly.** Go to bed 15 minutes later for a few nights before the change.
- **Get morning sunlight.** Light in the morning helps reset your body clock.
- **Keep meals on schedule.** Eat at your usual times by the clock.
- **Help kids and pets adjust.** Move their routines a little at a time.
- **Drive carefully.** Evening commutes will be darker, so watch for people walking.

## Is daylight saving time going away?

Many people want to stop changing clocks. Congress has debated bills to make daylight saving time permanent, and some states have passed laws that would do so if Congress allows it. But as of this writing, **no change has become federal law**, so clocks will still change in 2026 and 2027.

## A quick history of daylight saving time

- **1918:** The U.S. first used daylight saving time during World War I to save fuel.
- **1942 to 1945:** It was used year-round during World War II, called "War Time."
- **1966:** The Uniform Time Act set national rules, but states could opt out.
- **2007:** The current schedule started, moving the change to March and November.

The main idea has always been to use more natural daylight in the evening.

## Does the time change affect your health?

Many people feel tired for a few days after a time change, especially in spring when we lose an hour. The fall change is usually easier, but it can still shift your sleep and mood, and the darker evenings can feel gloomy.

Simple ways to feel better:

- Get outside in the morning light.
- Keep your bedtime and wake time steady.
- Avoid big meals, caffeine, and screens right before bed.
- Take a short walk in the afternoon when it is still light.

If you feel very low as the days get shorter, talk to a doctor. Some people get a type of seasonal depression in fall and winter.

## Driving safety after the clocks fall back

Evenings get dark earlier after the time change, often right when people drive home from work or school.

- Turn on your headlights earlier.
- Watch for people walking and biking, especially children.
- Clean your headlights and windshield.
- Slow down in neighborhoods and near schools.
- Wear bright or reflective clothing if you walk or run in the evening.

## Time change tips for kids and pets

**Kids:** Move bedtime by 10 to 15 minutes a day for a few days before the change. Keep morning routines the same, and use blackout curtains if it is too bright at bedtime.

**Pets:** Dogs and cats are used to meals and walks at the same time. Shift their feeding and walk times a little each day so the change does not confuse them.

## How to count hours on the change night

When clocks fall back, the hour from 1:00 to 2:00 a.m. happens twice. If you work an overnight shift, that night may be 9 hours instead of 8. Our [hours and minutes calculator](/tools/hours-and-minutes-calculator) can help you add up the time.

## The bottom line

Daylight saving time ends on **Sunday, November 1, 2026, at 2:00 a.m.** Clocks go back one hour, and you get an extra hour of sleep. Your phone and computer will change on their own, but set your microwave, oven, car, and wall clocks by hand. Clocks spring forward again on **Sunday, March 14, 2027**. Use the time change as a reminder to test your smoke alarms.
