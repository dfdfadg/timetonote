Yes, hurricane shutters are usually worth it **if you live where hurricanes or strong tropical storms hit.** When a window breaks in a storm, wind and rain rush in and can cause roof and interior damage that costs far more than the shutters. Most homeowners pay about **$1,400 to $5,800** for shutters, depending on the type and how many windows they cover.

The right choice depends on your budget, how often storms hit your area, and whether you can close shutters yourself before a storm.

## Hurricane shutter types and costs

| Type | Typical cost (2026) | Setup before a storm | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storm panels (aluminum or polycarbonate) | About $10 to $20 per sq ft | Put up by hand, can take hours | Tight budgets |
| Accordion shutters | About $15 to $30 per sq ft | Slide closed and lock | Good value, easy use |
| Roll-down shutters | About $25 to $60 per sq ft | Push a button or crank | Easiest, very strong |
| Colonial or Bahama shutters | Varies, often mid to high | Close and latch | Looks, some shade |
| Impact windows (instead of shutters) | About $800 to $1,400 per window in Florida | None, always protected | Long-term, all-in-one |

Costs vary by city, window size, and installer. Get at least three quotes.

## Why shutters matter

When a strong wind breaks a window, air pressure inside the house can build up quickly. That pressure can push up on the roof from inside and lead to much bigger damage. Keeping windows and doors covered helps keep the "envelope" of the house sealed.

Shutters also help protect against:

- Flying debris like branches and patio furniture
- Rain driven sideways into the house
- Break-ins when the house is empty

## Shutters vs impact windows

| | Hurricane shutters | Impact windows |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lower | Much higher |
| Protection | Strong when closed | Always on |
| Setup | Must close before a storm | None |
| Noise and energy | Little help | Blocks noise, often more efficient |
| Looks | Visible hardware | Look like normal windows |
| Best if | Budget matters, you are home to close them | You travel, or want a long-term upgrade |

Some homeowners mix them: impact windows on hard-to-reach upper floors, and shutters on the rest.

## Are they worth it for you?

**Shutters are usually worth it if:**

- You live in a coastal or hurricane-prone state
- Your windows are not impact-rated
- Your insurer offers a discount for storm protection
- You plan to stay in the home for several years

**They may not be worth it if:**

- You live far inland where hurricanes rarely reach
- Your home already has impact windows
- Local rules or your HOA limit what you can install

## Insurance and permits

- **Insurance discounts:** In some states, especially Florida, a **wind mitigation inspection** documents your storm protection and can lower your premium. Ask your agent what counts.
- **Permits:** Many coastal towns require a permit for permanent shutters and impact windows, and products may need to meet local building codes.
- **Product ratings:** Look for shutters tested for your area's wind and impact standards, such as Florida Product Approval or Miami-Dade approval in South Florida.

## Tips before hurricane season

- **Test your shutters** at the start of the season, and oil tracks and locks.
- **Label storm panels** by window so they go up quickly.
- **Store hardware** like bolts and wing nuts in a labeled bag.
- **Trim trees** near the house to reduce flying debris. Our [calculator for tree trimming prices](/tools/tree-trimming-cost-calculator) gives an estimate.
- **Check your roof,** since wind can lift damaged shingles. See [signs your roof needs repair](/signs-your-roof-needs-repair-or-replacement).
- **Prepare for outages.** Our guide on [what to do in a power outage](/what-to-do-if-there-is-a-power-outage) covers food, generators, and emergency kits.

## The bottom line

Hurricane shutters are worth it for most homes in hurricane-prone areas. Storm panels are the cheapest, accordion shutters give good value, and roll-down shutters are the easiest to use. Impact windows cost more but protect all the time without any setup. Check local permit rules, ask your insurer about discounts, and get several quotes before you decide.
