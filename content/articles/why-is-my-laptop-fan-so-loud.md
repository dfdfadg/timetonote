A laptop fan gets loud because **the laptop is hot and the fan is working hard to cool it**. That is normal during games, video calls, and big downloads. But if the fan is loud **all the time**, even with nothing open, the usual causes are **background programs**, **dust in the vents**, a **blocked air flow**, or a **performance setting** that pushes the processor hard.

Here is how to find the cause and quiet your fan on **Windows** and **Mac**.

## Is the noise normal?

| Situation | Normal? |
| --- | --- |
| Loud during games, video editing, or video calls | Yes |
| Loud for a few minutes after turning on or waking up | Usually, updates may be running |
| Loud while charging and working hard | Usually |
| Loud all the time with nothing open | No, something is using power or airflow is blocked |
| Grinding, rattling, or clicking sounds | No, the fan may be damaged |
| Very hot and shutting down | No, get it checked |

## 9 reasons your laptop fan is loud (and how to fix them)

### 1. Programs are running in the background

Even when you are not doing anything, your laptop may be installing updates, scanning for viruses, syncing files, or indexing your search. This keeps the processor busy, which makes heat.

**Fix:**

- **Windows:** Press **Ctrl + Shift + Esc** to open **Task Manager**. Click the **CPU** column to sort it. Close anything using a lot that you do not need.
- **Mac:** Open **Activity Monitor** (in Applications > Utilities) and click the **CPU** tab.

If Windows Update or an antivirus scan is running, let it finish. The fan should calm down afterward.

### 2. Too many browser tabs

Browsers use a lot of processing power, especially with video, ads, and web apps. A single heavy tab can keep your fan spinning.

**Fix:** Close tabs you do not need, and turn on your browser's memory saver setting. Our guide on [cutting down Chrome memory use](/why-is-google-chrome-so-slow) shows how.

### 3. The vents are blocked

Laptops pull in cool air and push out hot air through vents, usually on the bottom, sides, or back. Using a laptop on a bed, pillow, couch, or your lap blocks those vents.

**Fix:** Use your laptop on a **hard, flat surface** like a desk or table. Keep vents at least a few inches away from walls and other objects.

### 4. Dust is clogging the fan

Over time, dust and pet hair build up in the vents and fan. The fan has to spin faster to move the same amount of air.

**Fix:**

1. Turn off and unplug the laptop.
2. Use a can of **compressed air** and blow **short bursts** into the vents at an angle.
3. Hold the can upright, and do not shake it.

For a deep cleaning inside the laptop, take it to a repair shop. Opening it yourself can void the warranty on some models.

### 5. The power mode is set to maximum performance

High-performance settings let the processor run faster and hotter, which makes the fan spin more.

**Fix:**

- **Windows:** Go to **Settings > System > Power & battery** and set **Power mode** to **Balanced** or **Best power efficiency** when you do not need full speed.
- **Mac:** Go to **System Settings > Battery** and try **Low Power Mode** for quiet everyday use.

Many laptop makers also have their own apps, like **Lenovo Vantage**, **HP Command Center**, or **MyASUS**, with a **quiet** or **cool** fan mode.

### 6. The room is hot

A hot room means hotter air going into the laptop. The fan has to work harder.

**Fix:** Keep the laptop out of direct sunlight, use it in a cooler room when you can, and consider a **cooling pad** for long gaming or work sessions.

### 7. Software or drivers are out of date

Updates can fix bugs that make the processor or fan work harder than needed. Laptop makers sometimes release **BIOS** or firmware updates that improve fan control.

**Fix:**

- **Windows:** Go to **Settings > Windows Update**.
- **Mac:** Go to **System Settings > General > Software Update**.
- Check your laptop maker's support app or website for driver and BIOS updates.

### 8. Malware is using your processor

Malicious software can run hidden programs that keep your processor busy and your fan loud.

**Fix:** On Windows, open **Windows Security** and run a **Full scan**. Only install programs from trusted websites and official app stores.

### 9. The fan or cooling system is worn out

If the fan **grinds, rattles, clicks, or squeals**, it may be damaged or its bearings may be worn. On older laptops, the **thermal paste** that helps move heat away from the processor can also dry out.

**Fix:** Take the laptop to a repair shop. Replacing a fan or thermal paste is usually quick for a technician.

## Gaming laptops

Gaming laptops have powerful chips that make a lot of heat, so loud fans during games are expected. To keep noise and heat down:

- Use the maker's app to pick a **balanced** or **quiet** mode when you are not gaming.
- **Cap the frame rate** in your games. Running at 60 frames per second instead of the highest possible makes much less heat.
- **Lower graphics settings** a little, like shadows and effects.
- Play on a desk with a **cooling pad**, and keep the back vents clear.
- Keep the laptop **plugged in** while gaming, but let it rest between long sessions.

## MacBook fan loud?

MacBooks with Apple chips often run quietly, and some models have no fan at all. If your MacBook's fan is loud:

- Check **Activity Monitor** for apps using a lot of CPU. Browser tabs and video apps are common causes.
- Make sure the vents are not blocked. On many models they are near the hinge.
- Restart the Mac to stop stuck processes.
- Keep macOS updated.
- If the fan stays loud with nothing running, contact Apple Support.

## Quiet fan checklist

1. Check Task Manager or Activity Monitor for busy programs.
2. Close extra browser tabs.
3. Use the laptop on a hard surface.
4. Blow dust out of the vents with compressed air.
5. Switch to a balanced or quiet power mode.
6. Install updates.
7. Run a virus scan.
8. Get it checked if you hear grinding or clicking.

## Loud fan and slow laptop?

If your laptop is loud **and** slow, it may be running hot and slowing itself down to cool off. This is called **thermal throttling**. Clearing dust, closing background programs, and freeing up storage usually help. Our guide on [speeding up a slow laptop](/why-is-my-laptop-so-slow) walks through the rest of the fixes.

## When to get help

Take your laptop to a repair shop if:

- The fan makes grinding, rattling, or clicking sounds
- The laptop gets too hot to touch or shuts off by itself
- The fan runs at full speed all the time, even right after turning on
- Cleaning the vents and closing programs do not help

## Key points

A loud fan usually means your laptop is working hard or cannot get rid of heat. Check for busy background programs, close extra tabs, keep the vents clear on a hard surface, and blow out dust with compressed air. Use a balanced power mode and keep your software updated. If the fan grinds or the laptop overheats, have it checked by a technician.
